Why tape still matters

Recording to tape isnt all that difficult. and really, in the end, its a fantastic way to force some creativity out of yourself. Limitations are completely underrated. in our very human and specifically american pursuit of "more" or "easier" (the goal of which i guess would be to eventually not have to do anything?) we have engineered the hell out of everything to get maximum ease of use, maximum array of choice, maximum control, maximum perfection.

Sure it sounds good but its absolutely paralizing. Boot up a DAW, that lets say you torrented, and immediately you have multiple orchetras worth of virtual instruments, an imposssibly large selection of effects, processors, manipulators and editing tools, and theres no skill required either, as lomg as you have a basic understadning of how to bop around on a macbook your a youtube video and a half away from being your own Brian Eno. Or whoever, Rolamd Kirk.

The perfect has become so attainable and the possibilities so endless that (ill speak only formyself here) i run into one of a couple of issues right away. What instrument do i pick? lets say i go with synth, my reasoning being a synth at least isnt as much of an emulation as an oboe, because a computer can do a lot of what a synth can and there is software made that make for wildly interesting and incredible digital software synthesizers.

But even then which do i choose? When you have every drum machine in the world what drum sounds do you use? what bass guitar do you emulate out of which amp and through what pedals? do you see what im getting at?

And even if i make a choice, at anypoint along the ay i can change my mind. Your brain starts to fall prey to a particular type of brain disease the kind where I end up 10 hours later with the same midi notes on the piano roll and a cramped hand and a noyjing of a song, and by them im numb to muisc, analyzed it to death, absolute paralyzation of choice leading to a complete lack of productivity, creativity and fun.

Now thats what it did to me, and i immediately knew i didnt wanna make music if a DAW come stock with everything you cvould ever want and theres even more and betterer plug ins sprinkled cyberspace infinitly and i have to have them all so i can finally make my music and suddenly its three weeks later, not a single second of music made, hard drive busting at the seems with pirated software of the best rack gear, analog synths, digital synths, effects units, compressors, amp and cab emulators and so on that a man could possibly want and thats about the point someone might find this page.

because perfection is boring when its so attainable, anyone patient can make a perfect, even beautiful song but does that a musciain make? does that a recording artist make? are you really even recording if you never input anything external into the system or are you jusst a computer engineer, moving digital midi around, until you hiy some arbitrary mark no one knows and damn if they care. at what point are you doing something closer to clerical work than recording music?

First tip: ditch the computer

If that rings true at all you gotta chuck the computer, you can come back to it once you know what you want but if you are just getting into recording your own music, my first tip would be chuck the dang camputer.

and then theyll say "well im sorry i dont have a million dollars for all the fancy crap u have shining in your shiny music making room nor do i have the spcace for a buncha toys plus the danged racket i'll make in the process!" well guess what? it aint expensive, and it aint loud, and it aint shiny, it aint even big. Well it can be, and is sometimes, but it doesnt have to be.

Im gunna walk you through geetting started recording without the danged computer so buckle up, its not gunna be the prettiest thing at first but who wants pretty anyway? you ready? openminded? lets go.

Limitations are a feature, not a bug. Keep the rig small, keep the decisions few, keep the joy alive.

If Not Computer, Than What?

Computer boy, with his little computer computing the day away. thats you. dont you feel silly and bad? like a silly little computer boy, clicking and touching anything you could ever want? well how do you like THIS:

the 4 Track

that thing is a multitrack cassette recorder, or as the kids used to call it a 4 track. it takes a cassette tape, and lets you record four tracks onto it, over one and other. in the same space, and time. are you freaking out? i bet you thought cassettes could only play one sound at a time, and the sound was a whole already well made and put together song. But no! well yes, but ALSO it can handle you recording yourself doing whatever dumb shit FOURTIMES. and to be clear, incase you still arent catching up, i mean four times at the same time. you can be john paul george and ringo, all in a row, and then listen back and itll sound like your own little beatles were all jamming together!!!

not impressed? okay let me stun you with some branding/technical terminology. this specifically is the MFP01 by the company Tascam, stick with me and youll learn to know that name better than your own mothers name or something. Tascam. Taz- like uhhh T-A-Z and CAM like the first part of camera. now im going to walk you through how to use this so called TAscam MFp01 and your gunna have to get creative because, well it doesnt have any sounds thats supposed to be your job. just follow my lead here okay? its of simple steps.

Ultimate beginners multitrack tape machine

how to make a song with the mfp01

  1. plug headphones into the red headphone jack
  2. crank monitor knob till u hear hiss, now pullback to midnight
  3. notice the single 1/4inch input jack, front in front right there, just the one.
  4. look at the face of the thing. not so scary eh? just 5 knobs, 4 wheel things 4 buttons, and a little tape player
  5. plug a synth, guitar, mic, bass, contact mic taped to a jar of pennie’s, oscillating delay pedal, reel to reel headphone out with sone radioplay recorded on it, micro cassette dictaphone u field recorded chiping birds onto, a drum machine u figured out plays a beat u like as long as u hit that special button or anything else that plugs, even ur damn iphone if u have one of them dongles
  6. actually unplug whichever of those things you landed on and grab any one of the boss effect pedals that are always in the vicinity of any electrical music equipment, plug the thing into that (make sure it’s 9 volt isn’t dead) then the pedal into four track
  7. click the button under the little wheel thing in track one, notice it start to blink red, understand that this means whatever u plugged into the input will later be controlled by that volume wheel and thatpan knob above that blinking light
  8. rewind to the front of the tape (find one at ur parents house, any tape even if it’s frank sinatra and his face is on the cover) and make sure u put tape (of the adhesive variety) over the holes in the top of the tape. if you check and the holes are filled with tabs disregard, that just means you either grabbed a cassette ur dad recorded a football game on or u actually bought a blank tape meant for recording which isn’t entirely necessary and if it slows u down whatsoever isn’t worth it. remember people, tapes don’t have to be blank to be recorded on
  9. press the counter reset and then hit record and play at the same time sn make sure one of the redlights is blinking
  10. lay down something hot or groovy or crazy or stupid to track one (you can also lay down something better described by a different adjective)
  11. push the button on the next track everytime you wanna keep ur take, and move the little wheel that controls the volume up only on the tracks you’ve previous laid down, keep it all the way down for the tracks you previous haven’t (i’m looking out for my fellow tab covering tape recyclers)
  12. rinse and repeat until ur out of tracks
  13. rewind and hit play, just play this time andmess wirh the wheels to get what they call in the biz a “mix”, use the knobs at the top of the tracks to move the sounds around ur head, when it sounds good (enough) stop moving stuff
  14. plug the two rca outs into ur tape deck or if you must into ur interface but at least use AUDACITY
  15. hit play and record on the tape deck and then hit play on the mfp01. move ur headphones to the tape deck to make sure the input or record level of the tape deck (or audacity/ur interface) isn’t too loud or too quiet, too guilt you’ll hear more hiss then ur song too loud you’ll get some saturation (could be nice on the tape deck) or if audacity disgusting digital distortion.

General rule

mixing to tape don’t be afraid to push the levels a bit, the red on the indicator is ur friend, mixing to digital avoid it at all costs, much easier to crank the volume later since digital doesn’t hiss.

you’ve now recorded mixed and mastered a song of ur very own. now you must do this u til you die or ull feel empty for some reason so…. repeat until you have a C60 of sounds you’ve made, scribble a name on the master tape in sharpie and write the track names on a receipt that’s laying around also put ur phone number. shove these into a padded envelope and put the mailing address of ur favorite tape label on the front. sprint to the post office because it’s 445 and get there at 455 covered in sweat and feeling terrible from staying up all night following these steps over and over. send tape via media mail. back home and kick ur feet up and wait for that phone to ring and the money to come in cuz guess what baby? it’s all gravy from here on out. welcome to being a fucking recording artist.
