Home Recording:
Then vs. Now

Home recording wasn't always the effortless, sunday stroll with your best gal coupla scoops of that delectable frozen cream perched every so daintily atop a thin hard waffle forced by the ice cream boy to be conical.

No, there was a time when home recording was by most, not even an idea that ever crossed their minds and to the few who wondered why, wondered if they could record serious multitrack music as a single individual. Well those early adopters were largely laughed at, then thrown a bunch of intimidating jargon that would make any non-music engineering dorks head spin. Signal to noise ratio this, generation-loss that, tape types, bias settings, demagging the things and so on and so forth because audio engineering and the musical recording process was supposed to be extremely difficult, extremely unattainable without the deep pockets of a record label, the pockets you wouldn't have any choice but to make deeper by giving them large percentages (in certain situations as large as 100 percent, which i find to be the largest percent) of any of the money made on anything you recorded ever, because they had us over the proverbial barrel.

A great example of the attitude towards home recording, as recently as 1984, is this interview with one of the pioneers of home recording, the most prolific home recording artists ever to live and, i believe, one of the best songwriters of all time, personal hero and constant inspiration of mine, the unparalleled R. Stevie Moore. He began home recording in the late '60s, and by this i mean multitracking, building songs one instrument at a time all alone, which is astonishing because there was no piece of equipment advertised as capable of doing that at the time. We'll get into how he achieved this, but watch the interviewer ask about this so-called "home recording", he even goes as far as to ask stevie if he started "recording in his home" as some sort of joke. Stevie, after being thrown by the incredulity of the dumb interviewer and the surprising disdain for even the idea of trying to record without spending a year's salary, gives a simple, poignant and genuine answer by saying, firstly, recording himself at his home is simpler, it doesn't require multiple people with clashing personalities and he says it's cheaper. at the time he had a cassette club and had released 109 albums of original music, a feat in prolificism only capable when the artist controls the means of production and distribution, and summing up his point he very soberly states that he does it for freedom. damn near brings a tear to my eye. ROLL IT!

If you are unfamiliar with R. Stevie Moore, I would start with something like Glad Music, Sheetrock or Delicate Tensions, two wonderful LPs released first in the 70s and 80s, but which have been recently (enough) reissued and should be available. I was lucky enough to release a tape for the man a while back on my tape label OJC Recordings, a later less appreciated release entitled Kaffeeklsch which i feel shows beautifully his range as a song writer, instrumentalist, vocalist, and home recordist. Check it out on the OJC bandcamp or Spotify or R. Stevie's bandcamp and if you dig it I (am pretty sure) have a couple extra copies, if interested email me at augie@otorlymern-electrical.com

Computerless Home Recording: Recording to Tape

Recording to tape isn't all that difficult. and really, in the end, it's a fantastic way to force some creativity out of yourself. Limitations are completely underrated as a tool artists can use to push themselves into more interesting, less comfortable, territory, or a quick fix to writer's block. in our very human (and more specifically american) pursuit of "more" or "easier" (the goal of which i guess would be to eventually not have to do anything?) we have engineered the hell out of everything to get maximum ease of use, maximum array of choice, maximum control, maximum perfection. WE have gone far past the point of making sure our biological needs are met, and at some point began enhancing the tools of the artist (maybe with the idea that if such and such genius work was made using only a two track and some microphones, imagine how amazing a piece that has no track or instrumental limits stuck to it would be!).

Sure it sounds good but it's absolutely paralyzing. Boot up a DAW, that, let's say, you torrented, and immediately for zero dollars you have multiple orchestras worth of virtual instruments, an impossibly large selection of effects, processors, manipulators and editing tools, and there's no skill required either (in the traditional sense of musical skill), as long as you have a basic understanding of how to bop around on a macbook you're a youtube video and a half away from being your own Brian Eno. Or whoever, Roland Kirk. Or maybe you are bad with computers, like an old guy, but wildly talented in the traditional musical sense. in that case there's a HUGE learning curve! and that's no fun! Back to the beginner with the torrented version of Ableton. let's say they are following some sort of youtube tutorial on how to make their favorite genre in ableton. Let's imagine step 1 is laying down the drum track. Once the midi writing or sequencing style is understood technically, and the beginner has a little 8 beat down, this is where the trouble comes in. When it's time to pick what actual kick snare and hihat sample they will use, and how to process each one individually, this is where i'm sure a lot of people accidentally stay up all night, chasing a non existent "perfect combo" for the non existent "perfect song." Even limited to their favorite genre, there are way too many possibilities and i bet at this first choice is where a lot of people get frustrated and quit.

When you have every drum machine in the world what drum sounds do you use? The perfect has become so attainable and the possibilities so endless that (i'll speak only for myself here) i run into one of a couple of issues right away. What instrument do i pick next? let's say i go with synth, my reasoning being a synth at least isn't as much of an emulation as an oboe, because a computer can do a lot of what a synth can and there is software made that make for wildly interesting and incredible digital software synthesizers.

But even then which do i choose? and then do i pick a preset or learn to program the softsynth myself? Let's say i decide to pick a preset, and i want it to act as the bass line of the song, i can go to the bass section of the presets and have a reasonable number of choices in the factory loaded patches. But what if i don't like any of em? if i reach out to the internet for help i could immediately have near infinite options again, by downloading or buying preset packs, then i'm stuck again, hunting for which preset bass synth sound of all the ones i just downloaded the perfect one for my perfect song. Let's say i get frustrated and say fuck it, i want it to be a software version of an electric bass guitar, that'll help me get rid of this analysious paralyzous.....or will it? what bass guitar do you emulate out of which amp and through what pedals? what plucking style do you want to use? standard tuning? do you see what i'm getting at?

And even if i make a choice, at any point along the way i can change my mind. Your brain starts to fall prey to a particular type of brain disease, the kind where I end up 10 hours later with the same midi notes on the piano roll, a cramped hand and a numb and broken ear having analyzed it to death. IF i'm not having fun and not making any music, why am i doing this?

at least that was my harrowing experience. because perfection is boring when it's so attainable, anyone patient can make a "perfect" song but does that a musician make? does that a recording artist make? are you really even recording if you never input anything external into the system, just steer ones and zeros around like a little computer boy? that's not music guy, that's being a computer boy and silly at that.

Perfection is boring when it's so attainable.

In our effort to achieve the perfect music making machine we forgot that so much of what forces the recording artist to think outside the box, innovate through problem solving, were (i mean are) the limitations that come with hardware and the finality that comes with committing something to tape.

First tip: ditch the computer

If that rings true at all you gotta chuck the computer, you can come back to it once you know what you want but if you are just getting into recording your own music, my first tip would be chuck the dang computer.

and you might be saying to me now "well i'm sorry i don't have a million dollars for all the fancy crap u have shining in your shiny music making room nor do i have the space for a buncha toys plus the danged racket i'll make in the process!" well guess what? it ain't expensive, and it ain't loud, and it ain't shiny, it ain't even big. Well it can be, and is sometimes, but it doesn't have to be.

i'm gunna walk you through getting started recording without the danged computer so buckle up, it's not gunna be the prettiest thing at first but who wants pretty anyway? you ready? open-minded? let's go.

Computer boy, with his little computer computing the day away. that's you. don't you feel silly and bad? like a silly little computer boy, clicking and touching anything you could ever want?

If Not Computer, Then What?

this is your run of the mill portable tape recorder. not as sporty or small as a walkman, not as banging or shoulder worthy as a boombox. right in the middle these are great as veritable notebooks, for getting ideas, chord progressions, words, down quickly, in their stock condition. they all come with a little condenser mic that compresses the shit outta everything and honestly sounds pretty good whether it's next to a speaker or ur whispering into it.

If ur a singer songwriter, a solo instrumentalist, recorded modular synth and vocal improvisations, this is sometimes all you need. but this page is about giving you the tools to go beyond capturing the sounds you can create live. Luckily, there's a pretty easy way to make this thing into a multitrack tape recorder. an unlimited multitrack tape recorder... although it comes at a bit of a price. without further ado i'll just let mr. Joey Agresta aka Joey Pizza Slice explain his technique he uses for his fantastic project Son of Salami. in quick summation before you watch the video below, you can remove the erase head with some pliers, or cover it with some tin foil, adhesive tape, a guitar pick.... as long as u prevent the erase head from touching the tape wiping the slate clean as it were every time you hit the record button (which is how tape machines typically function, so unwanted takes or previously recorded material can be recorded over) you can keep stacking layers over each other endlessly. it's tricky and takes some practice, but it can yield really great results and force you to get creative.

Eraserheadless Tape Recording

joey pizza slice aka son of salami aka salami jr. aka joey agresta shares his ingenious computerless recording method with us by demonstrating, track by track, how he makes a song.

the 4 Track

that thing is a multitrack cassette recorder, or as the kids used to call it a 4 track. it takes a cassette tape, and lets you record four tracks onto it, over one and other. in the same space, and time. are you freaking out? i bet you thought cassettes could only play one sound at a time, and the sound was a whole already well made and put together song kinda sound. But no! well yes, but ALSO it can handle you recording yourself doing whatever dumb shit FOUR TIMES. and to be clear, in case you still aren't catching up, i mean four times at the same time. you can be john paul george and ringo, all in a row, and then listen back and it'll sound like your own little beatles were all jamming together!!!

not impressed? okay let me stun you with some branding/technical terminology. this specifically is the MFP01 by the company Tascam, stick with me and you'll learn to know that name better than your own mothers name or something. Tascam. Taz- like uhhh T-A-Z and CAM like the first part of camera. now i'm going to walk you through how to use this so-called Tascam MFP01 and your gunna have to get creative because, well it doesn't have any sounds that's supposed to be your job. just follow my lead here okay? it's of simple steps.

Ultimate beginners multitrack tape machine and how to make your first computerless song in ? easy steps!!!!

how to make a song with the mfp01

  1. plug headphones into the red headphone jack
  2. crank monitor knob till u hear hiss, now pull back to midnight
  3. notice the single 1/4-inch input jack, front in front right there, just the one.
  4. look at the face of the thing. not so scary eh? just 5 knobs, 4 wheel things, 4 buttons, and a little tape player
  5. plug a synth, guitar, mic, bass, contact mic taped to a jar of pennies, oscillating delay pedal, reel to reel headphone out with some radio play recorded on it, micro cassette dictaphone u field recorded chirping birds onto, a drum machine u figured out plays a beat u like as long as u hit that special button or anything else that plugs, even ur damn iphone if u have one of them dongles
  6. actually unplug whichever of those things you landed on and grab any one of the boss effect pedals that are always in the vicinity of any electrical music equipment, plug the thing into that (make sure its 9-volt isn’t dead) then the pedal into four track
  7. click the button under the little wheel thing in track one, notice it starts to blink red, understand that this means whatever u plugged into the input will later be controlled by that volume wheel and that pan knob above that blinking light
  8. rewind to the front of the tape (find one at ur parents house, any tape even if it’s frank sinatra and his face is on the cover) and make sure u put tape (of the adhesive variety) over the holes in the top of the tape. if you check and the holes are filled with tabs disregard, that just means you either grabbed a cassette ur dad recorded a football game on or u actually bought a blank tape meant for recording which isn’t entirely necessary and if it slows u down whatsoever isn’t worth it. remember people, tapes don’t have to be blank to be recorded on
  9. press the counter reset and then hit record and play at the same time and make sure one of the red lights is blinking
  10. lay down something hot or groovy or crazy or stupid to track one (you can also lay down something better described by a different adjective)
  11. push the button on the next track every time you wanna keep ur take, and move the little wheel that controls the volume up only on the tracks you’ve previously laid down, keep it all the way down for the tracks you previously haven’t (i’m looking out for my fellow tab covering tape recyclers)
  12. rinse and repeat until ur out of tracks
  13. rewind and hit play, just play this time and mess with the wheels to get what they call in the biz a “mix”, use the knobs at the top of the tracks to move the sounds around ur head, when it sounds good (enough) stop moving stuff
  14. plug the two rca outs into ur tape deck or if you must into ur interface but at least use AUDACITY
  15. hit play and record on the tape deck and then hit play on the mfp01. move ur headphones to the tape deck to make sure the input or record level of the tape deck (or audacity/ur interface) isn’t too loud or too quiet, too quiet you’ll hear more hiss than ur song, too loud you’ll get some saturation (could be nice on the tape deck) or if audacity disgusting digital distortion.

General rule

mixing to tape don’t be afraid to push the levels a bit, the red on the indicator is ur friend, mixing to digital avoid it at all costs, much easier to crank the volume later since digital doesn’t hiss.

you’ve now recorded mixed and mastered a song of ur very own. now you must do this until you die or you'll feel empty for some reason so…. repeat until you have a C60 of sounds you’ve made, scribble a name on the master tape in sharpie and write the track names on a receipt that’s laying around also put ur phone number. shove these into a padded envelope and put the mailing address of ur favorite tape label on the front. sprint to the post office because it’s 445 and get there at 455 covered in sweat and feeling terrible from staying up all night following these steps over and over. send tape via media mail. get back home and kick ur feet up and wait for that phone to ring and the money to come in cuz guess what baby? it’s all gravy from here on out. welcome to being a fucking recording artist.
