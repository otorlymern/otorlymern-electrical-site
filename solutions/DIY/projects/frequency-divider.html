Seven-Stage Frequency Divider

4024 add-on for a synthesizer VCO/VCA

← Back to DIY Audio & Video Projects

Description

A CD4024 binary counter provides seven square-wave suboctaves from a VCO square wave. A DIP switch selects a division and a small potentiometer attenuates the signal before it is returned to the synthesizer VCA.

Open original source PDF ↗

Materials
  • CD4024 counter and the small set of supporting parts shown in the original schematic
  • Small PCB, miniature potentiometer, DIP switch, wire, and mounting hardware
  • A square-wave VCO signal and compatible VCA input
  • Optional Schmitt trigger if the source oscillator does not provide a suitable square wave
Step-by-step guide
  1. Plan the connection points.

    The article is written for a PAiA Gnome. Identify its VCO square-wave source and VCA input; for another instrument, confirm signal level, grounding, and input compatibility first.

  2. Make the board and control openings.

    Follow the article’s board artwork and mechanical drawing. Drill/file the control openings before mounting the board.

  3. Populate and inspect the divider.

    Install the CD4024 and supporting components, checking IC and capacitor polarity against the diagram.

  4. Wire the selector and level control.

    Connect the DIP switch to select the divide-by-2 through divide-by-128 outputs. Wire the miniature pot as shown to attenuate the chosen output.

  5. Connect input and return.

    Route the VCO square wave into the divider and its output back to the VCA. Keep the ground reference common; the article uses direct coupling.

  6. Test each division.

    Start with the lowest level setting and a simple oscillator tone. Toggle one DIP selection at a time and verify progressively lower square-wave pitches before closing the instrument.

The article’s mechanical instructions target the PAiA Gnome. Do not drill or connect another instrument until its internal layout and signal levels are confirmed.
