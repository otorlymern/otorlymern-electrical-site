Seven-Stage Frequency Divider

4024 add-on for a synthesizer VCO/VCA

← Back to DIY Audio & Video Projects

Description

A CD4024 binary counter provides seven square-wave suboctaves from a VCO square wave. A DIP switch selects a division and a small potentiometer attenuates the signal before it is returned to the synthesizer VCA.

Materials & complete parts schedule

John Blacet’s original linked extract contains only printed p. 21 and stops before the circuit. The archive’s two-page continuation copy was also checked: PDF p. 2 / printed p. 31 supplies Figure 4 and the complete parts list. That recovered schematic is embedded below.

As required Insulated wire, insulating sheet, mounting adhesive or suitable hardware Source suggests cardboard/foam insulation and epoxy/RTV for switch mounting. Confirm compatibility and clearance before using adhesive in an instrument.

Tools: meter, soldering equipment, hand drill/file for the original Gnome installation, and ideally a frequency counter or scope. Remove power before opening the instrument. The source circuit uses the Gnome’s 9 V rail; the general CD4024 operating range quoted in the article does not permit raising the supply above the listed capacitors’ 10 V rating.

Construction, commissioning & operation
  1. Plan the host installation without cutting anything.

    Identify Q6 collector, TS-2 lugs 3/5 and S7 lug 4 only if the host is the relevant PAiA Gnome. For another synth, obtain its service information, identify an above-ground square-wave source and a compatible audio return, and verify voltage range first. The source specifies direct coupling and mentions a Schmitt-trigger interface as an alternative, but provides no such interface circuit.

  2. Make and inspect the small board.

    Use perfboard wired to Figure 4, or reproduce Figure 2 only after checking package pitch and mirror orientation. Drill/file panel openings with the host electronics protected or removed; clear all metal debris before reassembly. The article does not provide a numerical cutout template.

  3. Fit resistors, socket and capacitors.

    Wire the 56 Ω supply resistor and 100 µF filter, reset/ground connections and output 150 kΩ/5 µF path. Observe both electrolytic polarities. With the IC removed, check continuity and absence of a supply short. Apply host power and measure pin 14 positive relative to pin 7, then switch off again before inserting the counter.

  4. Wire the pot and selector outside the instrument.

    Make the common bus across all seven switch contacts and connect it to the pot’s high end. Connect the pot’s grounded end and wiper as in the net table. The source’s physical orientation puts the pot lugs toward the switch: its “top” lug is ground, center is wiper, bottom receives the common bus. Prefer functional labels if your pot is oriented differently.

  5. Finish the seven output wires.

    Wire CD4024 pins 3/4/5/6/9/11/12 to the seven selector poles in order. Check no solder bridge joins adjacent counter outputs. Confirm one and only one selected pole at a time with a meter before connecting the VCO. A rotary selector is not part of this source design.

  6. Connect the four host leads.

    Source lead lengths are 4½ inches for +9 V, 5 inches for VCO, 3 inches for VCA and 2 inches from the grounded pot lug to TS-2 lug 5. These are routing lengths for the original Gnome, not mandatory lengths for another enclosure. Insulate the board back, secure the assembly, and prevent pot/switch terminals contacting the chassis.

  7. Test the counter before listening.

    Use a known above-ground square wave referenced to divider ground. Measure the clock at pin 1 and each output against the table below. Pin 2 must remain low for counting. CD4024 counts negative-going input transitions; the original source is not a bipolar audio input or a guaranteed 5 V-to-9 V level converter.

  8. Bring up the audio return.

    Set the 10 kΩ level to minimum, choose only /2, and connect the output to the compatible VCA point. Increase level slowly. Move through the seven divisions one at a time, opening the previous switch before closing another. Then mix the resulting suboctave with the original voice through the host’s normal signal path.

  9. Close the enclosure after full clearance checks.

    Verify no loose wire, conductive debris, exposed common bus or uninsulated foil can touch chassis or another circuit. Confirm selector and pot cannot mechanically foul the host board. Repeat the seven-output listening test after assembly.

Expected measurements

The following 128 Hz test values are calculated binary ratios, not measurements of a completed board. Use a scope or frequency counter; a DC meter only shows an average on a running output. High outputs approach the counter’s local VDD, low outputs approach ground; VDD is the voltage after the 56 Ω resistor.

CD4024 pin Switch pole Division / octaves down Output for 128 Hz clock
12 7 /2 · 1 64 Hz
11 6 /4 · 2 32 Hz
9 5 /8 · 3 16 Hz
6 4 /16 · 4 8 Hz
5 3 /32 · 5 4 Hz
4 2 /64 · 6 2 Hz
3 1 /128 · 7 1 Hz

Do not close several DIP poles to “mix” outputs. Figure 4 directly joins selected CMOS output pins through the common bus; simultaneous high and low outputs would contend. The historical prose mentions choosing/mixing divisions, but does not provide isolation resistors or a safe multiple-output mixer. For this reconstruction use one pole at a time. An isolated mixer would be a separate redesign, not an undocumented addition to the source.

Troubleshooting

Symptom Checks
All outputs stopped Check local VDD after 56 Ω, IC orientation, reset pin 2 low, common ground, and input waveform at pin 1.
Counting erratic or pitch unstable Inspect square-wave edges/logic swing, ground routing and direct coupling. Do not assume audio amplitude equals valid CMOS logic. The optional Schmitt interface is not specified by this article.
Counter works but no VCA signal Check selected pole, common bus, pot wiper, 150 kΩ, 5 µF polarity and TS-2 lug 3 return.
Output weak Deep divisions may be below audible range, the pot is an attenuator, and 150 kΩ feeds the host VCA rather than a power amplifier. Test /2 at a higher VCO pitch; do not remove the series resistor as a troubleshooting shortcut.
Supply droops or IC warms Power off immediately; check shorts, reversed capacitors and multiple closed selector poles. Never troubleshoot a hot CMOS chip by increasing the supply.

Pin-by-pin wiring

View both DIP packages from above, with the notch/dot matching the drawing. A DIP switch’s printed numbering may differ: check its continuity pairs instead of assuming physical left/right equals electrical pole number.

Net Connection
Supply Gnome +9 V → 56 Ω → CD4024 pin 14. 100 µF positive → pin 14; negative → ground. CD4024 pins 2 (reset) and 7 (VSS) → ground. Pins 8, 10, 13 are NC and remain unconnected.
Clock VCO square-wave source at Gnome Q6 collector → CD4024 pin 1 directly. Clock-source and divider grounds must be common; no series input coupling capacitor is present.
Selector poles 1–7 Connect CD4024 pins 3, 4, 5, 6, 9, 11, 12 respectively to switch pins 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7 as Figure 4 shows. These select /128, /64, /32, /16, /8, /4, /2 respectively.
Selector common Bus switch pins 8–14 together (the other side of all seven contacts). That bus → high end of 10 kΩ pot. Low end of pot → ground.
Output Pot wiper → 150 kΩ → positive terminal of 5 µF capacitor; capacitor negative → VCA connection. Do not reverse the output capacitor.
Gnome return points Ground → TS-2 lug 5. +9 V source → S7 lug 4. Output-capacitor return → TS-2 lug 3 (VCA). Q6 collector is the VCO connection. These are Gnome-specific references from Figure 3, not universal synth jack pinouts.
