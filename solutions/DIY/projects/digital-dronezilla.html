Digital Dronezilla

Recovered original circuit · four-voice digital drone

← Back to DIY Audio & Video Projects

Description

A four-voice just-intonation drone concept. The article explains combining a reference frequency, phase-locked loops, and programmable binary division to derive related pitches; it includes a programming truth table.

Step-by-step guide

What the recovered continuation changes

Printed p. 33 contains the missing Fig. B, a third IC (CD4010 buffer), component values and a regulated 5 V supply instruction. It also identifies four square-wave voice outputs and explains that the capacitor near CD4046 pin 9 controls pitch-change settling. The author found capacitors of 100 µF or more introduced unwanted behavior; the drawn value is 50 µF. Those are historical observations, not measurements of a new OES prototype. full issue PDF p. 35 / printed p. 33, Fig. B and continuation.

Four-voice arrangement

Duplicate the one-voice circuit four times, sharing the reference source and supply while keeping each voice’s four load selections and output separate. The four outputs are individually amplified in the author’s performance setup; the schematic does not show a four-channel electronic mixer. Do not short active outputs together.

Source-visible wiring landmarks

CD4046 VCO timing 0.0047 µF is drawn at pins 6/7; pin 11 has 1 kΩ to ground. Preserve the source’s remaining timing/enable connections in the image rather than substituting a generic PLL application circuit.
CD4046 loop filter Pin 13 connects through 1 MΩ to pin 9. Pin 9 also connects through 60 kΩ to the positive end of 50 µF, whose negative end is grounded.

  1. Inventory one voice before duplicating.

    Use exactly one 8281, CD4010 and CD4046 with the six resistors and five capacitors listed above. Lay out one complete voice first. Leave the other three voices unpopulated until it works. Fig. B is the historical circuit; there is no published PCB artwork.

  2. Assemble and verify +5 V distribution.

    Use a well-regulated isolated 5 V supply as the author directs. With ICs out, check ground and +5 V at every drawn supply pin, including both CD4010 positive pins, and verify no rail short. Put the 0.01 µF disc capacitor adjacent to the TTL 8281 supply connections. The source gives no current budget, so select protection based on the actual devices, not a fictional published figure.

  3. Build the 8281 network and buffer loop.

    Fit the 120 pF polystyrene capacitor, 2.2 kΩ and 1 kΩ network at divider pin 1. Fit the load contact pairs and the CD4010 exactly as Fig. B. Trace every loop wire end-to-end, distinguishing crossovers from dots before inserting the ICs. The additional buffer is part of the recovered design and must not be omitted as it was from the original partial description.

  4. Build the PLL oscillator and loop filter.

    Fit the 0.0047 µF timing capacitor and 1 kΩ timing resistor, then the pin-13-to-pin-9 1 MΩ path and the 60 kΩ/50 µF series branch to ground. Observe the 50 µF positive end toward the 60 kΩ resistor. The author links this capacitance to settling/glide; start with the drawn 50 µF rather than a very large experimental value.

  5. Wire reference and output independently.

    Feed the reference through the drawn 0.022 µF/22 kΩ series input. Do not connect reference directly to the output or load contacts. Label the VCO output and divided feedback separately. The article supplies no reference-amplitude specification, so qualify the chosen source against the actual PLL input before applying it.

  6. Verify supply and lock before listening.

    Insert sockets/ICs with power removed and grounded-iron/static precautions. Recheck 5 V at the powered devices. Select a defined load state and measure reference, oscillator output and returned feedback with a scope/counter. The returned divided signal should agree with the reference when locked, while the VCO node supplies the selected multiple. The article has no numeric settling-time or allowable jitter tolerance.

  7. Identify the physical bit order by measurement.

    With a stable 440 Hz reference, select all highs: the table predicts 440 Hz. Select all lows: it predicts 7040 Hz. Then identify which physical contact corresponds to each printed bit by changing one selection at a time, recording the frequency and comparing the complete table. This avoids inventing an unprinted 8281 bit-order legend. These are predicted targets, not observed measurements.

  8. Test all sixteen codes and their transitions.

    Record each output/reference ratio after it settles. Pitch changes may glide, phase-shift or alter pulse width, as the continuation discusses. Do not assume a failed high-ratio lock can be fixed merely by increasing loop capacitance: first confirm the timing, buffer loop, supply and source waveform.

  9. Add protected monitoring and duplicate.

    Use an appropriate attenuated/DC-blocked or buffered interface before connecting each square-wave output to audio equipment; this interface is not drawn in Fig. B. Once one voice passes all states, duplicate it three times. Check shared-reference loading and whether one voice’s programming disturbs another; retain separate output paths.

  10. Record the as-built configuration.

    Document original IC types/package markings, all component values, reference amplitude/frequency, supply current measured on the real build, physical-bit mapping, settling observations and output interface. There are no trimmers or formal calibration procedure in the source; the acceptance checks above are explicit circuit-derived bench work, not a claimed source calibration recipe.

Fault isolation

Symptom Specific check
No operation Verify 5 V at all three devices, both positive CD4010 pins, TTL bypass capacitor and socket orientation.
VCO oscillates but never locks Check the 8281/CD4010 feedback routing, reference coupling, the 1 MΩ loop connection and 60 kΩ/50 µF branch before changing values.
Counter reads 440 Hz for every code Make sure the counter is on the marked VCO output, not the divided feedback node.
Programming order seems reversed Use N = 16 − code. The physical bit order is not labeled; establish it using the complete frequency table.
High ratios fail Check oscillator timing/range, logic-interface operation and supply/reference integrity using actual device documentation.
Very slow or unusual transition behavior Verify the 50 µF value/polarity; the author specifically warns that 100 µF or greater can create undesired behavior.
Four voices interact Check reference fan-out, common rail/ground routing and output loading; do not combine outputs directly.
Monitor signal is too large or has DC Disconnect it and qualify the separate audio interface. Fig. B has no line-level conditioning.

Remaining limits after successful recovery

Remaining unspecified items are reference-oscillator design/input amplitude, supply current rating, switching hardware and a printed physical-bit legend, unused-pin handling details for modern variants, PCB layout, audio-output protection/attenuation and measured lock/settling tolerances.
