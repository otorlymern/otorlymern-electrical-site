Don Lancaster · 1977

Psych-Tone Melody Synthesizer

Full vintage electronics construction article

← Back to DIY Audio & Video Projects

Description

A self-generating melody instrument built around a 63-state tune computer, selectable patterns, tempo and voice circuits, and attack/sustain/decay shaping. The article describes 28 controls, six voices, and a large set of sequence combinations.

Open original source PDF ↗

Materials
  • Use the complete seven-block parts list in the original article; it includes logic ICs, transistors, a transformer, selector switches, controls, speaker, and chassis parts
  • Perfboard or the article’s board, sockets, wiring harness, enclosure, and control labels
  • A safe isolated AC/DC supply built to the source schematic; do not improvise the transformer or mains wiring
Step-by-step guide
  1. Read the full parts list and schematic first.

    The source names several period-specific ICs and custom interlocked switches. Verify availability, package pinouts, and safe equivalents before buying parts.

  2. Plan the seven functional blocks.

    Build and label the tempo generator, tune computer, tune selectors, tone generator, voice/filter section, envelope controls, and output/power section as separate areas.

  3. Assemble the logic and timing blocks.

    Follow the original board layout and schematic; keep IC orientation and switch wiring traceable. Check each block for shorts before connecting it to the next.

  4. Build the tone and voice path.

    Wire the selected sequence into the tone generator, then the voice/filter and envelope controls. Keep the audio output disconnected until the supply has been checked.

  5. Finish the controls and chassis.

    Mount the 28 controls/switches and speaker according to the article’s mechanical drawings. Bundle and label harnesses so the selector wiring can be traced.

  6. Preliminary checkout.

    Follow the article’s checkout sequence: verify wiring, turn on the monitor and play controls, confirm a sequence starts, then connect an external amplifier and test each voice control at low volume.

This is a complex 1977 build with period parts and transformer-powered circuitry. Treat the original power-supply diagram and full parts list as mandatory; this summary is not a substitute for them.
