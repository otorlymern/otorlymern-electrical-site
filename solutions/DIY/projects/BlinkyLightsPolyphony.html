Craig Anderton · Polyphony

Blinky Lights

Audio-reactive LED project

← Back to DIY Audio & Video Projects

Description

One CMOS binary counter and three decoders drive 24 LEDs in patterns at related rates. An op-amp clock, LFO control, or audio input can vary the display speed, making it a simple bridge between sound and visual art.

Select parts suitable for the actual 9 V circuit; capacitors need voltage margin above the voltage across them. Workshop tools: grounded soldering equipment, meter and ideally a scope. A plug-in isolated regulated 9 V supply or battery avoids building the article’s mains-connected supply sketch.

Construction, commissioning & operation
  1. Design the display in three banks of eight.

    Mark which eight LEDs belong to IC3, IC4 and IC5. Straight rows, concentric rings or a deliberately scattered pattern are all source suggestions. Mounting a bank in reverse physical order changes apparent motion without changing the electrical binary decode. Leave room for separate common cathode returns and all 24 individual anode wires.

  2. Wire and verify low-voltage power first.

    Use a regulated isolated 9 V DC source. Fit sockets and rail wiring; check pin 14/pin 7 on LM3900 and CD4024, and pin 16/pin 8 on each CD4028 before installing chips. Check pin 11 on each decoder is grounded and the counter reset pin 2 is grounded. Power off and discharge capacitors before inserting ICs.

  3. Build the counter and first LED bank.

    Wire the IC3 A/B/C inputs from the first three counter outputs and its eight LED anodes in the sequence above. Fit R1 as that bank’s common 270 Ω return. Add R12 to the counter input. Inspect for accidental ground on decoder output pin 7: on a 4028 pin 7 is an output, unlike the 4024 ground pin.

  4. Add the middle and slow banks.

    Use the table’s shifted counter-output groups for IC4 and IC5. Fit R2 and R3 separately. Keep the 24 anode wires labeled; a bank’s eight LEDs may be distant from the controller board. Check every LED’s polarity before blaming a missing count.

  5. Build the LM3900 voltage-controlled clock.

    Assemble IC1A, Q1, R6/R9/R11/R16/R17/R18, C2 and R14 from Figure 5. Confirm Q1’s actual lead arrangement from its own manufacturer data. Keep timing and high-value resistor nodes clean and short. Leave external control sources disconnected for the initial fixed-bias clock check.

  6. Commission the basic display.

    Apply 9 V using current limiting and monitor the rail voltage/current. Scope IC1 pin 4 and IC2 pin 1 for a changing clock. At slow enough rate, each bank should have one lit LED, with IC4 advancing at half the speed of IC3 and IC5 at half IC4. The source reports normal total current below 40 mA; a substantial excess, a hot chip or many simultaneously illuminated LEDs calls for immediate power-off and inspection.

  7. Add and test the slow LFO.

    Build IC1C/D and R5/R7/R8/R10/R13/C3. Confirm C3 positive is connected to pin 10. Scope pin 10 for a slowly changing waveform, then patch pin 10 to R18’s control input. Adjust R18 for the preferred excursion from individually visible steps toward a blurred display. The source supplies no guaranteed LFO period or absolute control-voltage range.

  8. Add and test the audio interface separately.

    Build IC1B, C1, R4 and R15, then disconnect the LFO control patch and connect amp out pin 5 to R18’s input. Use a modest compatible audio signal, starting attenuated. The source says a microphone or guitar pickup can drive the sensitive interface. Reduce input level if the display only jumps between very slow and maximum speed.

  9. Adjust control depth and optional bias.

    R18 controls the modulation depth/top rate. The audio attenuator and R18 interact; set the input so signal peaks give the fastest desired flashing, not continuous saturation. For the ambient-light option, a photoresistor can be put across R11; the source says brighter light raises the rate, but provides no specific photoresistor value.

  10. Mount after electrical tests pass.

    Use spacers for a sandwich display/controller arrangement or brackets for separate boards. Insulate all underside conductors, strain-relieve the supply/audio/control leads and ensure the LED panel cannot short board wiring. Repeat fixed-clock, LFO and audio-mode tests after mounting. Do not connect relay coils or lamps directly to decoder outputs.

IC2 pins 12, 11, 9, 6, 5 Successive binary divisions /2, /4, /8, /16, /32 of the input clock. Each successive signal changes at half the preceding frequency.

IC1 pin 10 Slowly varying LFO output; connecting it to R18 should vary clock rate, not directly address LEDs.
IC1 pin 5 Audio interface output should respond to changing input; the source describes a pseudo-envelope response, not a calibrated envelope voltage.

These are circuit-derived checks, not a claim that a newly assembled unit has been measured. The source does not state scope amplitudes, timing tolerances, exact flash rates or a transistor-base calibration voltage.

9 V versus 12 V and power safety

The article is designed for 9 V; at 12 V it suggests changing R11 from 1 MΩ to about 2.2 MΩ. Alternatively, replace R11 with a 5 or 10 MΩ pot: disconnect control inputs, start at maximum resistance, reduce until stepping starts to speed up, then back off for the slowest usable rate. It says R13 may also need trimming at other voltages but gives no replacement value. These are source-described modifications, not necessary commissioning steps at regulated 9 V.

Figure 7 is a historical transformer/rectifier/dropper sketch, not a fully specified modern supply project: transformer secondary and dropper values are not given. This guide therefore uses an isolated low-voltage regulated supply rather than inventing those values. The article’s final transistor/reed-relay suggestion likewise gives no driver circuit or coil/protection specifications; it is not an instruction to connect mains loads.

Troubleshooting

Symptom Check before changing component values
All dark Check 9 V, IC orientation, decoder pin 11 low, bank cathode return resistors and LED polarity.
One LED stays lit per bank Check clock at pin 4/pin 1 and reset pin 2 low. This is different from all-dark and usually means no count advancement.
Some steps missing or wrong order Check A/B/C bit order and the 4028 output pin sequence; inspect 24 anode wires and reversed LEDs.
All banks move at the same rate Recheck shifted input groups: middle bank starts at Q2, slow bank at Q3. Do not feed all banks from the same three bits.
LFO does not change speed Verify pin 10 reaches R18 upper end, R18 wiper reaches R6, C3 polarity and the LFO bias/feedback parts.
Audio gives only slow/fast switching Reduce input with the compatible attenuator or source level, then reset R18. The source explicitly warns about interface saturation.
No clock at regulated 9 V Check Q1 E/B/C orientation, C2, R9/R14 and R16/R17 values before trying optional R11/R13 bias modifications.
Brightness / supply current abnormal Check three separate 270 Ω returns and only one LED selected per bank. Do not lower resistors to compensate for a high-power or unsuitable LED load.

Display and controller schematics

Power and display wiring

Use +9 V and ground, not ±9 V. IC1 LM3900 pin 14 → +9 V, pin 7 → ground. IC2 CD4024 pin 14 → +9 V; pins 2 and 7 → ground; pin 1 is clock input and has R12 (1 MΩ) to ground. On each CD4028, pin 16 → +9 V; pin 8 → ground; D input pin 11 → ground. Unused counter outputs and decoder outputs 8/9 need no load. This is one counter with overlapping groups of output bits, not three cascaded counters.

Decoder A input pin 10 B input pin 13 C input pin 12 Relative advancement
IC3, fastest IC2 pin 12 (Q1) IC2 pin 11 (Q2) IC2 pin 9 (Q3) One LED position per counter input event
IC4, middle IC2 pin 11 (Q2) IC2 pin 9 (Q3) IC2 pin 6 (Q4) One position per two input events
IC5, slowest IC2 pin 9 (Q3) IC2 pin 6 (Q4) IC2 pin 5 (Q5) One position per four input events

For each decoder, output numbers 0–7 are pins 3, 14, 2, 15, 1, 6, 7, 4 respectively. Connect each of those pins to a separate LED anode. Join only that bank’s eight cathodes and return them to ground through its own 270 Ω resistor (R1 for IC3, R2 for IC4, R3 for IC5). Do not join all three cathode banks before one shared resistor. Only one LED per bank is normally selected at a time. The original drawing directly drives the LEDs from CMOS; it does not provide transistor drivers for large LED loads.

Optional audio attenuator

Figure 3 puts a 1 kΩ pot across the source with its low end grounded; its wiper feeds amp input through 10 kΩ. This is not a universally safe modern speaker tap. Bridged/class-D or floating speaker outputs must not be grounded this way. Prefer a known compatible low-level audio source; do not attach unknown powered speaker terminals, mains or a headphone output with incompatible loading. The original has no protection or isolation design for those cases.
