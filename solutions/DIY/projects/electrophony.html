Electrophony

Patch study · not a parts-level kit

← Back to DIY Audio & Video Projects

Description

This article proposes “electrophony”: creating the perception of several independent musical voices from a smaller set of synthesizer sources. Its examples are patch and performance techniques rather than a discrete circuit with a bill of materials.

Open original source PDF ↗

Materials
  • Two VCOs and two sample-and-hold/noise paths for the illustrated patch
  • A pair of filters that can be adjusted near self-oscillation
  • Mixer and/or balanced modulator for combining the sources
  • A keyboard, sequencer, or sub-audio square-wave sources for pitch control
  • Patch cables and an audio mixer for monitoring
Step-by-step guide
  1. Choose the article’s two-voice patch.

    Use the diagram in the source as the routing map. Start with two low-frequency VCOs tuned an octave apart.

  2. Create evolving overtone groups.

    Route each noise source through its sample-and-hold path and filter. Raise each filter’s resonance close to oscillation, then set levels conservatively.

  3. Listen to the sources separately.

    Monitor the VCOs and filtered outputs one at a time; adjust pitch and resonance until each layer is clear before combining them.

  4. Add the pitch-control approach.

    The article also describes cycling a sequencer through selected pitches or using several slightly detuned sub-audio square waves to make long changing patterns.

  5. Combine, record, and refine.

    Mix the parts or follow the balanced-modulator example from the article. Change one control at a time and document which combinations create the perceived extra voices.

This is a synthesis/patching article, not a standalone circuit construction plan. It does not provide a component BOM or wiring schematic for building a new instrument.
