Build Your Own Reverb

Acoustic effects build · no powered circuit required

← Back to DIY Audio & Video Projects

Description

Make an inexpensive physical reverb from a speaker, a spring or resonant plate, and a piezo contact microphone. The speaker excites the object; the contact mic captures its vibration as a return signal.

Open original source PDF ↗

Materials
  • Piezo disc/contact microphone
  • Shielded audio cable and 1/4-inch plug
  • Small loudspeaker and amplifier/mixer send
  • Slinky spring, metal plate, or other resonant object
  • Spring clip, mic stand/boom, tape, and optional Plast Dip coating
  • Headphones for checking the return
Step-by-step guide
  1. Make and test a contact mic.

    Attach shield to the piezo’s brass rim and the center conductor to the ceramic element as shown. Insulate the connections and plug it into a high-impedance guitar input or suitable preamp.

  2. Mount the resonator.

    For the spring version, secure one end of a Slinky inside a speaker cone and suspend the other end from a stand. Keep the spring stretched but not rigid.

  3. Attach the pickup.

    Clip the piezo contact mic to the free end of the spring. Keep the cable short and shielded; the source recommends staying under about 20 feet.

  4. Check the return before feeding audio.

    Tap or pluck the spring and listen through headphones. Set the return gain low, then confirm the pickup captures the spring without excessive hum.

  5. Create the reverb send/return.

    Send a quiet test signal through the speaker and blend the contact-mic return at the mixer. Increase send and return gradually.

  6. Tune the acoustic effect.

    Change spring tension, speaker level, pickup position, or damping. The article also describes plate, cymbal, and other object variants.

This is an acoustic effect build. Keep speaker output and mixer return at low levels during initial tests to avoid feedback.
