Digital Pattern Generator

CMOS sequencer build

← Back to DIY Audio & Video Projects

Description

A CMOS up/down counter turns one clock into four related step divisions. Four level controls feed a summed control voltage, so the pattern can change direction, run from a gate or trigger, and produce uneven rhythmic phrasing. The article describes a buildable circuit and a small set of controls.

Open original source PDF ↗

Materials
  • IC1: 4516 CMOS up/down counter
  • IC2: 4013 CMOS flip-flop; IC3: 4001 CMOS quad NOR gate
  • IC5: 741, 5558, or 4136 op amp; IC6: 4069 CMOS hex inverter
  • Resistors, 1/4 watt: 6 × 1 kΩ, 2 × 10 kΩ, 1 × 20 kΩ, 4 × 100 kΩ, 2 × 1 MΩ
  • Linear potentiometers: 1 × 5 MΩ, 5 × 50 kΩ, 1 × 10 kΩ
  • Capacitors: 1 × 1 µF mylar, 1 × 270 pF polystyrene or ceramic, 3 × 0.047 or 0.05 µF ceramic, 2 × 1.0 µF electrolytic
  • Miscellaneous: 4 × 1N4198 diodes, 1 SPST toggle switch, 4 1/8-inch jacks, and 4 LEDs
  • Regulated supply: the article recommends ±10 to ±15 V for the op amps, never above 15 V; follow its supply and bypass-capacitor schematic.
Step-by-step guide
  1. Study the schematic and mark the power rails.

    Identify IC1–IC6, the clock, four divided outputs, level controls, trigger/gate input, direction/run controls, and LED indicators before wiring. Use the original scanned schematic for every resistor and capacitor value.

  2. Build the clock and counter section first.

    Assemble the 4069 clock and 4516 counter with the 4013/4001 control logic. Keep CMOS devices protected from static and confirm pin orientation against the diagram.

  3. Add the four level paths and output summer.

    Wire the four controls to their separate timing divisions and combine them through the diode network and op-amp buffers shown in the article.

  4. Wire switches and external control.

    Add run, up/down, and trigger/gate controls as drawn. The article uses a positive pulse for a trigger or a sustained positive level for continuous gated sequences.

  5. Power up and test one block at a time.

    Use the regulated rails specified in the article. Verify the clock and counter outputs with LEDs or a meter before connecting the summed output to a VCO.

  6. Set the levels and try external clocking.

    Confirm the four divisions change at 1×, 2×, 4×, and 8× relationships as shown by the LEDs. The article also describes substituting an external fast-edge clock through a 10 kΩ resistor; follow its schematic and input-level guidance.

The article’s final page contains the complete parts list. Verify every pin connection against its scanned schematic, and follow the power-rail and bypass-capacitor notes before powering the build.
