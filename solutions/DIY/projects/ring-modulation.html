Ring Modulation Alternatives

Patch guide · source contains diagrams

← Back to DIY Audio & Video Projects

Description

A practical exploration of ring-modulator sounds beyond clangorous tones. The article uses control-wave shape, frequency, amplitude, and sequenced voltages to create tremolo, accents, and rhythmic changes.

Open original source PDF ↗

Materials
  • A ring modulator or balanced modulator with carrier and signal inputs
  • Two VCOs, or a VCO plus a sub-audio oscillator/sequencer
  • Mixer and headphones or speakers
  • Patch cables; use the article diagrams to match the chosen setup
Step-by-step guide
  1. Start with the basic two-oscillator patch.

    Connect one VCO to the signal input and the other to the carrier input as in the first diagram. Begin at low levels.

  2. Explore slow carrier changes.

    Lower the carrier into the sub-audio range and listen for amplitude modulation/tremolo. Sweep the carrier rate and waveform.

  3. Try ramps and pulse waves.

    Compare the ramp and pulse examples in the article. Their changing and abrupt voltage edges create different accent patterns.

  4. Sequence the carrier.

    Use stepped control voltages to change the carrier pitch or level. Follow the article’s sequencer examples to create repeated accents and rhythms.

  5. Document useful settings.

    Record oscillator waveforms, rates, levels, and sequence steps for patches worth keeping. The diagrams are examples, not a parts-level circuit design.

The supplied article is a patching discussion with diagrams. It does not specify a component-level ring-modulator build or a full parts list.
