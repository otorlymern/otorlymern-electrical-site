Anderton Flanger

Analog delay pedal construction article

← Back to DIY Audio & Video Projects

Description

A compact analog-delay guitar flanger using a SAD-1024 delay line and supporting clock, filter, and mixing circuitry. The source explains delay-line behavior, component selection, construction, and setup.

Materials

Pedal Flanger inventory

This is the three-part Pedal Flanger, not the separate Chorus-Delay article appended to the same file. Use PDF pp. 1–3 (Guitar Player, February–April 1980, printed pp. 123, 124 and 126). PDF pp. 4–9 describe a different design. The BOM below belongs to the pedal circuit: source PDF p. 2.

Fixed resistors: ¼ W, 5% preferred. Capacitors: source minimum 15 V working voltage; use a rating above the actual voltage across each capacitor. Keep the specified 2 µF and 5 µF values in the baseline inventory rather than silently substituting modern preferred values.

Step-by-step guide

Readiness and circuit map

The audio and both clock-control schematics are included below. This is a vintage BBD build requiring layout work and calibration, not a modern PCB kit. No verified replacement for the SAD-1024 or 4136 is established here. The circuit has manual delay control; the source does not provide an automatic sweep LFO.

  1. Plan the three-IC version before buying the rare delay chip.

    Build the March audio circuit plus April Fig. 1 CD4047 and Fig. 2 local control. Select Fig. 3 only if a remote pedal is required; that adds a fourth IC. Keep the 4047 and optional 4007 adjacent to the SAD-1024, with the clock traces away from the input amplifier, pots and switches. The source provides a schematic but no PCB artwork for this particular pedal. source PDF p. 3

  2. Check matched resistances and semiconductor identity.

    Measure R8–R16. Use the specified 11 kΩ resistors; the article permits a group of 10 kΩ resistors only if selected to fall within 5% of one another. Socket the SAD-1024 and clock CMOS and leave them in antistatic packaging until checks are complete. A 4136 has a different pin arrangement from many current quad op amps; do not insert a TL074 or other substitute into this wiring. source PDF p. 2

  3. Assemble and verify the isolated supply first.

    With ICs removed, verify +15 V and −15 V relative to the circuit 0 V, and confirm that no rail is shorted. In the audio schematic the 4136 uses pin 11 for +15 V and pin 7 for −15 V. The CD4047 runs between +15 V at pin 14 and 0 V at pin 7, not across the full 30 V bipolar supply. The delay section uses +15 V and 0 V as drawn. Check every socket against the inline figures before insertion. source PDF p. 2; source PDF p. 3

  4. Build the input amplifier and dry branch.

    J1 signal passes through C6 and R23 into the input stage. R26 sets gain, with C1 providing high-frequency feedback shaping. The dry branch reaches the output mixer through R14 and S2. Shield the lead from J1 to the board, return its sleeve to audio ground, and do not use the enclosure as the only ground conductor. source PDF p. 2; source PDF p. 3

  5. Build the delay input and bias network.

    Fit the R8–R10/C5 conditioning stage, C8, R24, the SAD-1024 socket, the R7 distortion/bias adjustment, and its decoupling parts. Match all SAD-1024 pin numbers to the drawing, including pins shown tied together: a symbol grouped above the package is not permission to leave a pin disconnected. Do not apply a guitar signal directly to the delay chip. source PDF p. 2

  6. Build the clock and selected timing control.

    In Fig. 1, 47 pF connects CD4047 timing pins 1 and 3. Control points C and D are the timing-resistance connections; the outputs at pins 10 and 11 are A and B and go directly to the matching SAD-1024 clock points. Fig. 2 places 4.7 kΩ in series with a 100 kΩ variable resistance, with the wiper strapped as drawn. Keep both clock wires short. source PDF p. 3

  7. If using remote control, wire the complete Fig. 3 network.

    The 4007 receives the pot-derived control at pin 10 through 100 kΩ, with the 1N914 and 1 µF network as drawn; the pot is bounded by 27 kΩ toward +15 V and 47 kΩ toward ground. Its timing-resistance path includes 1 kΩ and 330 kΩ. Mount the 4007 by the 4047, not out in the remote pedal. Start with the published values; only if the useful sweep is inadequate does the article suggest reducing 27 kΩ for the high-frequency end or 47 kΩ for the low-frequency end. They interact. source PDF p. 3

  8. Finish the clock-cancellation, filter and mix stages.

    R2 combines the delay outputs to suppress clock residue. C9 feeds the R12/R13/C3/C4 low-pass filter around the next 4136 section. C10 feeds feedback R5 and C11 feeds depth R4. Wire both poles of S1 exactly as the phase-reversing drawing, not as a bypass switch. The output mixer uses R15/C2; C12 feeds output-level control R6 and J2. source PDF p. 2

  9. Finish ground and shield wiring before powering.

    Use a single-point grounding approach where practical, short power returns and short audio leads. The author specifically calls for shielded wiring between S2 and IC1 pin 14 as well as at J1. Where the supply also feeds other modules, add the local reservoir across the 4047 supply noted in the inventory. Inspect capacitor polarity, solder bridges and the S1 cross-connections with power disconnected. source PDF p. 3

  10. Bring up clock and delayed path at low volume.

    Insert ICs with power disconnected and static precautions. Confirm both clock outputs switch before expecting delayed audio. The article does not state an exact acceptable clock-frequency range or supply-current limit: do not invent a frequency target or raise the supply to force operation. Keep the amplifier low; feedback oscillation can be loud. source PDF p. 3

  11. Calibrate R7 and gain R26 together.

    Open S2 for vibrato/delayed-only operation. Turn R4 fully up, R5/R6/R26 fully down, and the 100 kΩ clock control halfway; S1 may be in either position. Connect instrument to J1 and amplifier to J2, power on, then bring R6 halfway. Play loud chords with R26 just above minimum. Adjust R7 for least distortion; increase R26 slightly and readjust R7. Repeat until clean sound is no longer obtainable, then back R26 off to the highest clean setting. If later playing distorts, recheck R7 and reduce R26 further. source PDF p. 3

  12. Trim clock rejection and verify the flange.

    With a scope, probe the wiper/midpoint of R2 and adjust for the least fuzzy waveform; without a scope leave R2 halfway. Close S2, keep R4 fully up, set R6 for a safe level and turn the clock control through its range. Raise R5 only to just below oscillation, then compare both S1 phases. The positive phase is described as sharper/metallic and the negative phase as rounder/tubular. Opening S2 should remove the dry branch, leaving vibrato rather than comb-filter flanging. source PDF p. 3

  13. Record settings before considering modifications.

    Verify the baseline first. For line-level input the author prefers R26 = 100 kΩ; an alternative is R23 = 470 kΩ, with more noise. Increased output uses double R15 and half C2. The experimental slapback option changes C3/C4 to 0.01 µF Mylar and clock timing to 220 pF, with explicitly limited fidelity. These are alternatives, not additional parts to install together. source PDF p. 3

Fault isolation

Symptom Check / action
No delayed sound with S2 open Check rails, both clock phases A/B, SAD bias R7, gain R26 and signal before/after C8. A dry-only signal with S2 closed does not prove the BBD works.
Delayed signal clips on loud chords Re-run the R7/R26 alternating calibration. Do not turn up R26 to compensate for a fault elsewhere.
Clock whistle or fuzzy residue Adjust R2 with a scope, shorten A/B wires, check low-pass parts, shield input/S2 leads and separate clock returns. Some bleed at long delay is a design limitation.
Effect sounds like vibrato, not flange S2 is open or its dry branch is miswired. Verify the R14 path and output summing node.
Loud squeal Reduce R5 immediately and amplifier level. Feedback beyond the threshold intentionally self-oscillates.
Only one feedback phase works Check both poles of S1 and their routes to R19/R20, not just switch continuity in one position.
Remote sweep reaches only part of the range Check 4007 wiring and control voltage first; only then consider the interacting 27 kΩ/47 kΩ adjustments.
Hum or interaction with other modules Verify ground routing and local 4047 reservoir. Do not connect a second supply rail or mains earth to an arbitrary signal node.

Source limits and acceptance record

The supply circuit/current specification, PCB layout and modern replacement qualification are not supplied. The article gives no numerical BBD bias target or clock-frequency acceptance band. This guide retains the published trim-by-distortion method rather than inventing them. Before closing the case, record both rails, observed clock operation, the clean-input setting, R2 position, each switch function and the feedback threshold. These are bench checks to perform, not claims that an OES prototype has been built.
