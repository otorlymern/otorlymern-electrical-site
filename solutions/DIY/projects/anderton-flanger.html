Anderton Flanger

Analog delay pedal construction article

← Back to DIY Audio & Video Projects

Description

A compact analog-delay guitar flanger using a SAD-1024 delay line and supporting clock, filter, and mixing circuitry. The source explains delay-line behavior, component selection, construction, and setup.

Open original source PDF ↗

Materials
  • SAD-1024 analog delay IC (vintage; confirm availability and compatibility of any substitute)
  • RC4136/XR4136 quad op amp and the remaining ICs listed in the original parts table
  • Resistors, capacitors, trimmers, jacks, DPDT/SPST switches, sockets, wire, and enclosure per the article’s BOM
  • Audio test source, amplifier, and oscilloscope or audio probe for setup
Step-by-step guide
  1. Check the complete article and parts table.

    The article spans a two-part design and relies on the SAD-1024. Confirm that the available scan includes the circuit pages you intend to build and verify any replacement delay IC before laying out a board.

  2. Build the power and clock sections.

    Assemble the supply and clock circuitry from the schematic. Keep the clock wiring short and separate from audio wiring to reduce noise bleed.

  3. Wire the delay and filter path.

    Install the delay IC with correct orientation, then connect the input conditioning, output low-pass filter, and feedback/mix path exactly as shown.

  4. Add controls and jacks.

    Wire delay/feedback/phase controls and the bypass/input/output hardware. Check jack grounding and switch contacts against the article.

  5. Bring up with a limited test signal.

    Verify supply rails first, then confirm the clock and dry/wet signal paths independently before closing the enclosure.

  6. Calibrate and listen.

    Follow the article’s trim/setup notes, then test short delay for flanging, slower delay for vibrato, and the feedback/phase options at low amplifier volume.

The delay IC is a vintage part, and a substitute changes the design. Do not assume pin compatibility; verify the clock, bias, and filter interfaces before substituting.
